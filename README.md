# NostalGem

Retro cable TV. 65 channels that run 24/7, with Westerns at the top of the dial, plus serials, noir,
creature features, cartoons, sitcoms, game shows, newsreels, Apollo films, *The Computer Chronicles* and
old-time radio streamed from the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org)'s public collections, and a block
of official YouTube channels from CH 50 up. Free and non-commercial: see the [Disclaimer](#disclaimer).

Like real broadcast TV, every channel follows a schedule set by the clock. Tune in and you join
whatever's already on, mid-scene, at the same moment as every other TV tuned to that channel.
Flip channels with the static and hiss. Check the guide. Fall asleep to *Gunsmoke*.

It's an original app in the spirit of channel-surfing sites like spelunkr.tv. It doesn't share their
code, branding or look.

## What it does

- **Live, synchronized schedule.** Every TV agrees on what's on, with no server involved: the schedule
  is worked out from the clock. Programs have an 8-second station break between them with an "up next" card.
- **Cards with character.** The up-next card in a station break, and the card while a channel's program
  loads, come in the channel family's own look and voice: a sundown for the westerns, venetian blinds and
  rain for noir, a full moon for horror, a starfield for science fiction, T-minus for Mission Control, a
  comic panel for the cartoons, speed lines for anime, a curtain for comedy, equalizer bars for music,
  title cards between sprocket holes for the silent era, a countdown leader for the newsreels and serials,
  a green terminal for the computer channels, marquee bulbs for the game shows, a lower third for talk
  and news, a dial for radio. Every TV in the same break sees the same card. Which channel is in which
  family is in `web/js/cards.js` (viewer-added channels go by their words); the words are in
  [`web/data/cards.json`](web/data/cards.json), for anyone to edit. The YouTube sponsor card keeps its
  late-night deadpan and works as before, with the channel family's scenery turned right down behind it
  and a line in the channel's voice now and then.
- **House bumpers.** About one station break in three (every break on Commercial Break) shows a station
  ID card instead of the plain up-next card — short original bumpers from `web/data/bumpers.json`,
  picked from the clock so every TV in the same break sees the same one.
- **Picture filters**, like an emulator's video filters: black & white, hard noir, sepia, Technicolor,
  **Colorized** (color for black-and-white films: cool shadows, skin tones, warm light; a punchier
  *80s TV* version, and two-strip Technicolor),
  *Sin City* (black and white with one color kept: red, blue, green, yellow, purple or cyan; mix two
  Sin City looks and both colors stay), color tints,
  VHS tape, Game Boy, 8-bit, low-res pixels, **ASCII** (green, color, or with **Matrix rain**; the
  characters shimmer as they play), vaporwave, outrun, thermal camera, night vision, dreamy glow,
  negative, **ASCII · 666** (red, drawn in 666s, 999s, crosses and stars) and **Stained glass** (the
  picture in the panes of a leaded window, in jewel colors, even for black-and-white films). **Mix with** puts a second color filter on top: ASCII + Vaporwave, Game Boy + Hard noir. Menu ▸ Picture & sound ▸ 🎨 Filter, or press **V** (a
  controller's Y, or 🎨 on the phone) to step through them. They're drawn by the browser over the
  picture, so they work on YouTube channels too: browsers won't filter YouTube's player directly (it's
  another site's page), so there the filter runs on a see-through layer above it. Older TV browsers
  without that get simpler versions. The pixel and ASCII looks are drawn on a canvas (the video drawn
  small, then scaled up by the graphics chip; stained glass fills each pane of a Voronoi window with the
  color of one pixel of a tiny copy), so they run at full speed on a TV; on YouTube they get
  lighter stand-ins, since YouTube's player can't be drawn. **Filter quality** (Auto, Full, Light): on
  Auto, a filter that makes the picture choppy on your TV switches itself to its light version (run on
  a small copy of the picture), and says so. **On a TV** (the TV app, or a TV's own browser) the filters
  keep to a TV's means: ASCII has 80 characters across and a new picture ten times a second, stained
  glass eight times a second, and the filters drawn by the graphics chip over the whole screen (Sin
  City, the tints, VHS, Colorized and the other Wild ones) start on their light version, which a TV's
  slow processor can keep up with; **Filter quality ▸ Full** gets the full ones back. A computer is left
  as it is. Measured, not guessed: see the TV performance pull request.
- **YouTube ads**: when YouTube puts an ad before a show, a late-night "word from our sponsors" card
  covers it with the sound off, and the show starts by itself when the ad is over. OK never restarts it.
- **Ambience**: lo-fi vinyl crackle, a rainy window, or tape hiss under the program (Menu ▸ Picture & sound).
- **Number pad** on screen for remotes without number buttons (TCL's Google TV remote has none):
  Menu ▸ 🔢 Number pad, then the arrows and OK. 1 5 2 GO is CH 15-2.
- **Channel surfing**: ▲▼, CH+/CH−, or type a number. There's TV snow and a hiss between channels
  (or just snow, or neither), and holding ▲ flips fast.
- **TV guide**: channels down the side and time across the top, with what's on later and a
  description of each program. OK tunes in.
- **Info banner**: title, year, episode, progress, time left, what's up next and the clock.
- **Pause and back to live.** Pausing time-shifts the program, "Start this program over" rewinds,
  and L (or the menu) rejoins live.
- **Radio channels** show an animated *ON THE AIR* card over the sunset.
- **Subtitles**: human-made subtitles first (English, Español or any language), then the Archive's
  auto-captions. The Subtítulos channel turns Spanish subtitles on by itself.
- **Favorites**: ★ a channel and set ▲▼ to surf only your favorites. You can also hide channels.
- **Sleep timer** (15–120 min) with a "this concludes our broadcast day" sign-off.
- **"Are you still watching?"** after four quiet hours, to save the Archive's bandwidth.
- **CRT effect** (off, light or heavy scanlines), **picture** (fit, fill or stretch), clock (12 or 24 hour),
  and a corner channel logo you can switch off.
- **📺 Screen shape: 4:3 for old CRT TVs** (Menu ▸ Picture & sound). A set plugged into an old 4:3 TV
  (through an HDMI-to-AV adapter, say) shows the whole app in a 4:3 box in the middle of the picture,
  black at the sides, so nothing is cut off or stretched: the guide, menus, the banner and the filters all
  fit inside it, and 4:3 films fill it. Off unless you turn it on; Widescreen puts it back. The phone
  remote's pointer keeps to the box as well, and in the TV app (the newest one: the page tells it the
  shape) so do the sites the link-out stations open, 777's arcade included. In the
  stylesheet, anything sized to the screen's width uses `var(--vw)` instead of `vw` so it follows the box.
- **It keeps going** when the Archive doesn't. A program that won't play is skipped on that TV for a
  day. If several fail in a row, it shows colour bars and *PLEASE STAND BY* and retries by itself.
- Sized for a TV across the room, inside overscan margins. It also works with a mouse, a keyboard,
  or a touch screen: big CH ▲▼ buttons on the right, a dock along the bottom (guide, info, pause,
  number pad, menu, back), and swipe up/down to change channel. With the guide or a menu open, drag it
  to move through it, and the dock becomes ▲ ▼ ◀ ▶ OK BACK (hold an arrow to keep going).
- **What's new**: after an update, a page says what changed since the version you last saw (a welcome
  on a first start); Menu ▸ 📰 What's new shows it again, with the version number.
- **📼 Video Club**: the Archive's feature films, checked out like tapes from a neighborhood video store.
  Sign up for a free **library card** (three short pages: how the club works, what the Internet Archive
  is, and the fine print, signed with your name), then take up to **two tapes** at a time for **three
  days** each. A tape you have out plays from where you stopped it; when you're done, **◀◀ rewind** it
  and **⏏ return** it. A tape still out when it's due goes back by itself, with no late fees. The card
  lives on that TV only: it isn't an archive.org account and doesn't borrow from the Archive's own
  lending library. Any channel that's all feature films becomes a shelf of its own in the club, so
  any film on it can be checked out like a tape. A film with a **clean cut** (below) has a *Play TV-safe
  cut* switch on its tape. Menu ▸ 📼 Video Club.
- **📚 Reading Room**: the Archive's scanned books, comics, manga and magazines, read like a book open on a
  desk under a reading lamp. On a TV it shows two pages side by side (the cover on its own); **manga reads
  right to left**; there's one page at a time, and *Fit width* for small print (▲▼ scroll). Day, sepia or
  night paper; type a number to jump to a page; series come as volumes. Shelves: manga, Golden Age
  comics, comics, pulp magazines, classic novels, children's classics, movie magazines, game and computer
  magazines, plus a search and *open an archive.org link*. Above them, **the club's catalog**: books and
  Golden Age comics checked one by one to be public domain (`web/data/library-catalog.json`), and a manga
  shelf that stays empty and says why. The same library card borrows up to **three
  books for two weeks**, stamped with a due date; a bookmark keeps your page. Books the Archive only lends
  (with an archive.org account) aren't on the shelves; opened from a link, they show a code to read them
  on archive.org. Menu ▸ 📚 Reading Room. Remote: ◀▶ turn, OK settings, BACK close; touch: tap the
  edges or swipe.
- **✂ Clean cuts**: a few classic films are great but for a scene or two that can't air. A skip list names
  those scenes by time (sexual content only: gore and violence are never cut), and the file is never
  touched. On a live channel the scene goes under a full-screen card, **EXTREMELY GRAPHIC SCENE IS
  PLAYING**, with the sound cut, and the film plays on underneath, so the clock and the guide stay right.
  A Video Club tape plays as it is unless you turn its **TV-safe cut** on (on the tape's box); then the
  scene is jumped over instead, with a word on screen. Times only ever come from a real viewing pass of the Archive's own file, so none ship yet:
  `web/data/skip-lists.json` is empty, and a pack can carry its own (see [PACKS.md](PACKS.md)).
- **📱 Phone remote**: Menu ▸ Phone remote shows a QR code; the phone opens it and becomes a
  remote for that TV — channels, volume, guide — over a direct connection. The pairing code is
  kept, so a paired phone reconnects after the TV restarts. Its **Mouse** page is a trackpad and
  keyboard for the TV (777's arcade, ASCII City, the YouTube player), and its **Channels** page sends a
  channel pack to the TV.
- **📲 Send to TV**: paste a YouTube or archive.org link on the phone remote (or share it to the remote on
  Android) and the TV plays it right away, in NostalGem. The phone moves through it, and BACK goes back to
  the channel. See [Send to TV](#-send-to-tv).
- **Search the Archive** builds a channel from words, Archive search terms, or a pasted archive.org link
  (one item or a whole collection). A channel number typed there just tunes that channel.

## Put it on your TCL TV

First check which kind of TCL you have (look at the remote, or Settings ▸ About):

| You see | Your TV runs | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A Google Assistant / mic button, Google TV home screen | **Google TV** (or Android TV) | Install the app (below) |
| An Alexa button, Fire TV home screen | **Fire TV** | Install the app (below); it's the same app |
| A Roku logo, purple Roku home screen | **Roku TV** | See [Roku](#roku-tv) |

### Google TV, Android TV and Fire TV: install the app

The app is a real TV app with its own tile on the home screen, and it works with the remote's
channel, play/pause, info, guide and coloured buttons.

1. **Make the APK once:** on GitHub, open **Actions ▸ Android TV app ▸ Run workflow** (leave
   *Publish a release* ticked). A few minutes later the repo has a release with `nostalgem.apk`.
2. **On the TV, turn on developer mode:** Settings ▸ System ▸ About ▸ click **Android TV OS build**
   seven times. (Fire TV: Settings ▸ My Fire TV ▸ About ▸ click the device name seven times.)
3. **Install Downloader** (by AFTVnews) from the TV's app store, then allow it to install apps:
   Settings ▸ Apps ▸ Security & restrictions ▸ Unknown sources ▸ Downloader (Fire TV: My Fire TV ▸
   Developer options ▸ Install unknown apps).
4. **In Downloader, enter:**
   `https://github.com/<you>/<repo>/releases/latest/download/nostalgem.apk`
   then choose **Install**. NostalGem appears in your apps. Move it to your favorites row.

The app runs the published site, so new channels and fixes reach the TV as soon as they're on GitHub
Pages, with no reinstall. Stations that are another site (777's arcade and the like) open right on the
TV in the app: press OK, play with the remote, a controller or the phone remote's Mouse page, and press
BACK on the remote (or hold Select on the controller) to come back. Reinstall only when the app itself changes: run the workflow again and use
the same link. Your favorites and settings are kept.

> The APK is signed with a key kept in this repo (`android-tv/keystore/`), so new builds install over
> old ones. That's fine for your own TV. Publishing to the Play Store would need a private key instead.

### Any TV with a web browser, and phones, tablets and computers

Turn on GitHub Pages (Settings ▸ Pages ▸ Source: **GitHub Actions**) and the site is published at
`https://<you>.github.io/<repo>/` on every push to `main`. Open it in any browser and press OK or
click to turn the TV on. On a phone, "Add to Home Screen" opens it full screen like an app.

Google TV also has **Chromecast built in**: in desktop Chrome, open the site, then ⋮ ▸ Cast ▸ your TV.

### Roku TV

Roku can't run web pages or Android apps, so the app above won't install there. For now you can
mirror a phone or Windows PC to it (Roku supports screen mirroring). A native Roku channel would need
rewriting in Roku's own language (BrightScript), which would be a separate project. Ask if you'd like that next.

### 🔎 Find a show, watch it now

Every channel runs on a schedule, but you don't have to wait for a program. **Menu ▸ 🔎 Find a show**
searches every channel's programs by title (*junji ito*, *lone ranger*, *popeye*) and lists where each
one is on and when it airs next. Pick one to watch it **from the start, right now**; the banner says
**ON DEMAND**, and when it ends (or **Menu ▸ Back to live**, or L) the channel's schedule carries on.
In the TV guide, OK on a program later in the day does the same. The phone remote's Search tab finds
shows too, with a real keyboard.

### ⚠ When something won't play

A program that can't play on your TV (removed from YouTube, blocked in your country, a damaged file,
a live stream that ended) is skipped with a short note, and skipped on that TV for a day. If YouTube
or the Internet Archive isn't reachable at all, the TV shows **PLEASE STAND BY** and names which one,
and tries again every 30 seconds. **Menu ▸ ⚠ Playback problems** lists what didn't play and why. That
is the place to look when a friend says "some channels didn't work". YouTube channels are checked
daily for the US; abroad, some videos may be blocked (the **Lineup check** workflow shows how many).

### 📱 Phone remote

Typing a search with a TV remote is no fun, so your phone can be the remote instead. On the TV, open
**Menu ▸ 📱 Phone remote** and scan the QR code with your phone's camera (or open
`theupdoge.github.io/Nostalgem/remote` and type the six-character code). You get a D-pad, CH ▲▼, a
number pad, pause / live / mute, what's on now with a progress bar, a channel list you can tap, and a
real keyboard for **Search the Archive**.

- Nothing to install. To keep it handy, use your browser's **Add to Home Screen**; it opens like an app.
- It works with the Android TV app, a TV's web browser, or a computer plugged into or casting to the TV.
  On the TV's Wi-Fi the two link up directly. When they can't (guest Wi-Fi, a VPN, "AP isolation" on
  the router, the phone on mobile data), they link through an encrypted relay instead, and the phone
  says **Connected · via relay**. If neither works, the phone shows how far each got (small print under
  the message).
- The TV keeps its code, so a paired phone reconnects by itself after the TV restarts. **New code** in
  the same dialog disconnects every phone paired before; **Turn off** stops listening.
- The QR code points at the GitHub Pages copy, so turn on Pages first: Settings ▸ Pages ▸ Source: **GitHub Actions**.

**🖱 Mouse: a trackpad and keyboard for the TV.** For the sites some stations open (777's arcade, ASCII
City) and anything else that wants a mouse or a keyboard:

- Drag on the trackpad and a pointer moves on the TV; tap to click. Two fingers scroll (over the guide
  or a menu, a row at a time). Hold still a moment, then move, to drag (a slider, a game).
- Keys: Esc, Tab, ⌫, Enter, the arrows, space, and Z X A S for games. Held keys stay down (and the
  arrows and ⌫ repeat) until you let go.
- **⌨ Keyboard** drops down a text field: what you type goes to the TV as you type it, corrections too.
- **In the TV app** (updated once for this: run the workflow, then the same Downloader link) the app draws the pointer over whatever is on the screen
  and presses there for real: NostalGem, the YouTube player, and the link-out stations' sites, which open
  over NostalGem so the phone stays linked. While a site is up, the phone's arrows and OK go to it, BACK
  steps back through it and then home, and CH, Guide or Menu come home and do what they say.
- **In a browser** the TV page draws the pointer and works its own screen: menus, the guide, buttons and
  text fields. A browser doesn't let one page press buttons in another, so it can't reach into the
  YouTube player or a link-out site (those open in their own tab).

**📦 Send a pack to the TV** (the phone's Channels page): pick a channel pack, a `.json` or a `.zip` of
them, from the phone's files. It goes to the TV over the same link (direct or relay), and the TV checks
it with its own pack loader and asks **Load these packs?**; a pack that doesn't check out is listed and
left out, and the phone shows the same list. OK on the remote loads them, and the phone says what went
on the air.

### 📲 Send to TV

Found something on your phone? Send it to the TV. On the phone remote, open **Search**, paste a link in
**Send to TV** and tap **▶ Play on TV**. The TV plays it right away, inside NostalGem, with your picture
filter and subtitles, on the channel it was on. The banner says **ON DEMAND · 📲 FROM YOUR PHONE**.
**BACK** (or LIVE, or changing channel) goes back to the schedule. Live channels aren't touched: every
other TV still sees the shared clock.

- **What it takes:** a YouTube video or playlist, and an archive.org film or recording
  (`archive.org/details/…`, or a link to one file of it). An item in parts, or a playlist, plays one after
  the other. A link with a time in it (`?t=90`) starts there.
- **From the phone** you can drag the bar to move through it, tap ⏪ 15 and 30 ⏩, step between parts with
  ⏮ ⏭, and pause. The bar is there for anything on demand: a Video Club tape and "watch from the start" too.
- **🌐 Search the Archive**, on the same page, looks up films and recordings by title from the phone. Tap
  one and the TV plays it.
- **If a link-out station's site is open** over NostalGem in the TV app (777's arcade, say), it closes when
  you send a link, and when you tap a channel on the phone.
- **Android: the Share menu.** With the remote added to the home screen (Chrome ▸ Add to Home Screen),
  **NG Remote** appears in other apps' Share menus. Share a video from YouTube or a page from archive.org
  and it lands in the Send to TV box; one tap plays it. It never plays by itself, so a link somebody else
  made can't put something on your TV. iPhones have no share targets for web apps, so there you paste the link.
- **What it won't play:** a YouTube video whose owner doesn't allow it outside YouTube; an Archive item
  that's only for signed-in members; adult material (the sexual words Search the Archive leaves out, and
  nothing else from that list); an item with no MP4 and no MP3. The phone says which. A whole archive.org
  collection or search isn't one program, so the phone offers **📡 New TV channel** for it instead.
- **Nothing new to set up.** It goes over the link the remote already has (direct, or the encrypted
  relay), with no accounts and nothing from Google's Cast. It isn't screen mirroring: to show the phone's
  whole screen, use the phone's own (Android: Quick Settings ▸ Cast or Screen cast; iPhone: Screen
  Mirroring). [CASTING.md](CASTING.md) has the reasons.

## Make it yours

### Add or change channels

Channels are defined in [`web/data/channels.json`](web/data/channels.json). Each one is an
Internet Archive [advanced search](https://archive.org/advancedsearch.php) plus a few limits:

```json
{
  "number": 1, "sub": 2, "slug": "spaghetti", "name": "Dollars Trilogy", "tagline": "A few films more",
  "icon": "🎺", "color": "#e07a5f", "kind": "video",
  "minMinutes": 60, "maxMinutes": 180, "maxItems": 40, "filesPerItem": 1,
  "sources": [{ "q": "collection:feature_films AND subject:\"spaghetti western\"", "rows": 60 }]
}
```

- `number` and `sub`: every built-in channel stays under 99. CH 1–29 are all taken, so a new channel
  goes on as a sub-channel of the one it fits with (`"number": 1, "sub": 2` is CH 1-2, typed 0 1 2).
  CH 30–49 are left for channels viewers add, 50–90 hold the YouTube sub-channels, and
  100–199 and 200–299 are for channel packs.
- `kind`: `video` (MP4) or `audio` (MP3, shown as radio).
- `filesPerItem`: how many episodes one Archive item (a whole series, or a collection) may add.
- `maxYear`: skip anything dated later. The default is 1977, because public-domain films are mostly
  from before 1978. Set `null` for sources that are free regardless of age (NASA, Creative Commons).
- `exclude`: words that keep a title off this channel. The global `exclude`, `excludeDescription`
  and `excludeFiles` lists keep adult titles, ripped commercial releases and a few hateful
  cartoons and films off every channel.

Then rebuild the lineup (Node 18+, needs internet):

```sh
npm run lineup                          # every channel, about 3 minutes
npm run lineup -- --only high-noon      # just one
npm run lineup -- --seed spring-2027    # same programs, new running order
```

That writes `web/data/lineup.json`, which is what the TV loads. Commit it and both the site and the
app pick it up.

### Channel packs

A channel pack is a small JSON file that adds channels on your own TV, without touching
the shipped lineup. From the Menu: **Load a channel pack**, then choose a `.json` file, or a
`.zip` holding several packs, or paste its text. Pack channels land on CH 30–49, in the
hundreds (CH 100–199), or on CH 200–299 and are saved on that TV only, like channels you add by search.

A pack defines one or more channels — a name, a number (30–49, 100–199, or 200–299), and an Archive source:
a collection search or explicit Archive item IDs. Nothing else is allowed: no YouTube, no
other sites, no direct video links. The full format is in [PACKS.md](PACKS.md), with an
example in [packs/example-pack.json](packs/example-pack.json) (a western-features channel
built from the same Archive query as CH 1 High Noon).

Two packs ship with the repo: [1980s Sci-Fi — Public Domain](packs/1980s-sci-fi-pd.json)
(CH 30: 33 public-domain sci-fi features; CH 31: 45 Shuttle-era NASA and space-age films)
and [1990s Horror — Public Domain](packs/1990s-horror-pd.json) (CH 32: 36 public-domain
horror features), plus a [library catalog](web/data/library-catalog.json) of public-domain
books and Golden Age comics, on the Reading Room's catalog shelves. The 1980s/90s features
themselves are still under copyright, so they can't ship in a public pack — those live
on your own TV by pack, if you choose.

Packs are your choice on your own TV. They never change `web/data/lineup.json`.

### House bumpers

The station breaks can show a house bumper — an Adult Swim-style station ID card — instead of the
plain up-next card. They're in [`web/data/bumpers.json`](web/data/bumpers.json): one card each, a
kicker on top, a big title, and a line underneath. Add your own, delete the ones you don't like, and
they'll air in the breaks (about one in three, and every break on Commercial Break, CH 14). The
picker is clock-based, like the schedule, so every TV in the same break shows the same bumper.
No video needed.

### YouTube channels

A channel can also air full episodes from **official** YouTube channels (the studio's or distributor's own
uploads), played through YouTube's own embedded player. In `web/data/channels.json`, give it `youtube`
sources instead of Archive `sources`:

```json
"youtube": [
  { "handle": "@SomeOfficialChannel", "match": "episode|ep\\.? ?\\d", "exclude": "trailer|teaser", "order": "oldest" },
  { "playlist": "PLxxxxxxxx", "order": "playlist" }
]
```

Channels can share a number as **sub-channels**, like digital TV: give them the same `number` and a
`sub` (1, 2, …) and they show as 50-1, 50-2. On the remote, 5 0 1 tunes 50-1; 5 0 on its own goes to
the first one after a moment. Sub-channels can go past 9: 6 0 1 0 tunes 60-10, and where there is one,
6 0 1 waits a moment for the 0 before tuning 60-1. A one-digit channel's sub-channel is typed with its 0,
as the guide writes it: 0 3 2 tunes 03-2 (and 3 2 still does, unless a CH 32 is on the dial). Three-digit
channels (packs on 100–299) are typed and shown as themselves: 1 0 1 is CH 101. 2 0 2 is CH 202 when a
pack owns it, and CH 20-2 when it doesn't. Channel up and down still reach CH 20-2 either way.

Only public, embeddable videos that aren't age-restricted, live, Shorts or blocked in the US go on the
air. Building them needs a YouTube Data API key: add it as the repository secret `YOUTUBE_API_KEY`
(Settings ▸ Secrets and variables ▸ Actions) and run **Actions ▸ Rebuild lineup**. That also runs every
Monday to pick up new uploads and drop removed ones. Locally: `YOUTUBE_API_KEY=… npm run lineup -- --youtube-only`.

```
  js/books.js, js/ui/reader.js the Reading Room: Archive books as page images, and the reader
  js/ui/stacks.js              the Reading Room's book box and due-date slip
  data/books.json              the Reading Room's shelves (Archive searches)
  data/library-catalog.json    the club's catalog: checked public-domain books and comics (the catalog shelves)
  data/skip-lists.json         clean cuts that ship (none yet: times come from a real viewing pass)
  js/packs.js                  channel packs: format, validator, loader (Menu ▸ Load a channel pack)
  js/zip.js                    reads a .zip of packs (stored or deflated files; its own inflate and CRC-32)
  js/main.js                   wires it all together
  data/channels.json           channel definitions
  data/bumpers.json            house bumpers for the station breaks
  data/lineup.json             built programs (from tools/build-lineup.mjs)
  packs/example-pack.json      an example pack (western features, CH 30)
  PACKS.md                     the channel-pack format
android-tv/                    Kotlin WebView app for Google TV / Android TV / Fire TV

tests/                         node:test unit tests + a Playwright smoke test
```

- **The schedule.** A channel is a loop of programs, each followed by a short break. The position in
  the loop is `(now − epoch + channel offset) mod loop length`, so the schedule needs nothing but a clock.
- **Playing.** Videos load with a `#t=` media fragment so they start near the live point, then seek to
  it exactly. The player corrects drift after buffering, waits out programs that run short, and reports
  files that won't play so they drop out of that channel for a day.
- **The Android app** opens the published site, so fixes and the daily lineup arrive without a
  reinstall. It also bundles `web/` into the APK and serves it from a private https origin through
  `WebViewAssetLoader`, and switches to that copy when the site can't be reached (no internet, or no
  answer in 15 seconds). That copy asks the website for the latest lineup (rebuilt daily) at start-up
  and when you come back to it after a few hours, keeps a copy for when the internet is down, and falls
  back to the lineup inside the APK. A `format` number in the
  lineup keeps an older app from loading a lineup it can't play. It adds the home-screen banner, keeps
  the screen on, and passes remote buttons the page can't see (CH±, media keys, Guide, Info,
  coloured keys, Back) to `window.NP`.
- **The phone remote.** The TV registers with the free PeerJS broker as `nostalgem-<code>`; the phone
  finds it there, and from then on the two talk directly over a WebRTC data channel (relayed through
  PeerJS's TURN server when a network won't allow a direct link). Browsers hide their own address
  behind a random `….local` name that the other device has to look up over multicast DNS, which a lot
  of home Wi-Fi (and any Android app that doesn't ask for multicast) never answers. So the TV app hands
  the page its real Wi-Fi address (`window.NostalGemTV`, offered only to this site's pages), and the
  TV sends the phone each hidden address twice: as the browser wrote it, and with the real one. The phone sends the same actions a
  key press makes; the TV sends back what's on. Only whitelisted actions are accepted. The Mouse page
  sends pointer moves (fractions of the TV's width), clicks, scrolls, keys and text; in the TV app the page
  hands them to the app (`MainActivity.kt`), which draws the pointer and injects real touches, mouse
  hovers, wheel scrolls and key presses into whatever view is in front. A pack sent from the phone goes
  in 12,000-character pieces, each answered, and the TV puts it through the same loader as a file picked
  on the TV.
- **The relay** (`js/relay.js`) runs alongside, for networks where the two can't reach each other. Both
  sides connect out over secure WebSockets to public MQTT brokers (EMQX, HiveMQ, Eclipse); the TV
  listens on all of them, the phone uses the first where the TV answers. The topic is a hash of the code
  and every message is AES-GCM-encrypted with a key made from the code, so the brokers see only noise.
  The phone moves to the direct link if that comes up later.
- **Older TVs.** The app sticks to ES2018 (a test enforces it), ships its own fonts, and uses no frameworks.
